## Overview

Bangkok’s royal temples, river crossings and Chinatown work best as a few connected outings. Keep the Old City together, leave breaks between interiors and plan your airport journey from the actual airport code.

Sources: [Grand Palace practical information](https://www.royalgrandpalace.th/en/visit/practical-information); [Wat Pho visitor information](https://watpho.org/en/home); [Bangkok destination guide](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Destinations/Provinces/Bangkok/219)

## History and identity

The Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew form the royal and religious core of Rattanakosin. Wat Pho sits nearby, while Wat Arun stands across the Chao Phraya: the river is part of the city’s geography, not just a view.

Sources: [Grand Palace practical information](https://www.royalgrandpalace.th/en/visit/practical-information); [Wat Pho visitor information](https://watpho.org/en/home); [Bangkok destination guide](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Destinations/Provinces/Bangkok/219)

## Worth seeing

### Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew

Make this one planned palace-complex visit. Check current entry arrangements and the dress code before leaving your base.

Sources: [Grand Palace practical information](https://www.royalgrandpalace.th/en/visit/practical-information)

### Wat Pho

Explore the temple beside the Grand Palace on a separate ticket. Leave room for the courtyards rather than adding it as a rushed photograph stop.

Sources: [Wat Pho visitor information](https://watpho.org/en/home)

### Wat Arun

Plan a separate visit on the opposite bank of the river. Confirm the crossing or rail-and-walk route you will use rather than assuming a ferry timetable.

Sources: [Bangkok destination guide](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Destinations/Provinces/Bangkok/219); [Grand Palace practical information](https://www.royalgrandpalace.th/en/visit/practical-information)

### Yaowarat

Explore Chinatown as its own street outing, using Wat Mangkon MRT for a useful approach. Food choices and stall operation vary; there is no fixed meal budget here.

Sources: [Bangkok destination guide](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Destinations/Provinces/Bangkok/219); [Old City and Yaowarat by MRT](https://www.tatnews.org/2024/06/exploring-bangkoks-old-city-and-yaowarat-is-super-easy-with-mrt-blue-line/)

## Choosing a base

### Old City and the Sanam Chai area

Compare a base here if the palace and temple cluster drives your visit. May suit: Fewer cross-city trips for these sights. Tradeoff: Check the exact station or pier connection and the property’s luggage access.

Sources: [Old City and Yaowarat by MRT](https://www.tatnews.org/2024/06/exploring-bangkoks-old-city-and-yaowarat-is-super-easy-with-mrt-blue-line/); [Grand Palace practical information](https://www.royalgrandpalace.th/en/visit/practical-information)

### Asok and Sukhumvit interchange area

Consider a station-linked base if rail connections are more important than being beside the temples. May suit: An interchange between BTS and MRT services. Tradeoff: The Old City remains a separate journey; do not assume every Sukhumvit address is near the interchange.

Sources: [BTS rail network map](https://www.bts.co.th/btsroutes/btsroutes.pdf); [Old City and Yaowarat by MRT](https://www.tatnews.org/2024/06/exploring-bangkoks-old-city-and-yaowarat-is-super-easy-with-mrt-blue-line/)

## Arrival and local transport

### Suvarnabhumi (BKK)

The Airport Rail Link runs towards Phaya Thai, with an MRT connection from the Makkasan area. Match the final leg to your base and check the current airport guide for service notices. This is not the same route as Don Mueang.

Sources: [Airport Rail Link connections](https://www.thailand.go.th/public/issue-focus-detail/001_01_107-2); [Suvarnabhumi airport guide](https://suvarnabhumi.airportthai.co.th/service/airport-guide?lang=th)

### Don Mueang (DMK)

DMK has Red Line and airport-bus options. A September government advisory described transport disruption: check current operator notices before relying on a service. No taxi availability is assumed.

Sources: [Don Mueang transport advisory, 27 September 2026](https://thailand.go.th/public/issue-focus-detail/don-mueang-airport-operating-normally)

### Old City, river and Chinatown

Use Sanam Chai for Old City planning and Wat Mangkon for Yaowarat. Wat Arun is across the river from the palace area: check the actual crossing or onward route, and allow breaks between temple visits.

Sources: [Old City and Yaowarat by MRT](https://www.tatnews.org/2024/06/exploring-bangkoks-old-city-and-yaowarat-is-super-easy-with-mrt-blue-line/); [Bangkok destination guide](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Destinations/Provinces/Bangkok/219); [Grand Palace practical information](https://www.royalgrandpalace.th/en/visit/practical-information)

## Suggested short-stay outlines

These are proposed editorial sequences, not bookable tours or operator-tested schedules. Short arrival/departure days may require moving or omitting stops. No opening time or journey duration is assumed.

### 2 nights in Bangkok

- Day 1: **Yaowarat** — Keep arrival-day plans flexible; choose food individually.
- Day 2: **Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew** — Follow the current dress and visitor rules. **Wat Pho** — Add this nearby temple only if you still want another interior.
- Day 3: **Wat Arun** — A separate river-side visit only if departure leaves time.

### 3 nights in Bangkok

- Day 1: **Yaowarat** — A flexible Chinatown outing after settling in.
- Day 2: **Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew** — Give the palace complex its own main outing.
- Day 3: **Wat Pho** — A slower temple visit; use the rest of the day for breaks.
- Day 4: **Wat Arun** — Only if airport timing permits; otherwise pair with day three after confirming the crossing.
