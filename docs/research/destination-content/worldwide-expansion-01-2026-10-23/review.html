Barcelona v3 · DRAFT

A developed existing record with no full itinerary; introduces a Mediterranean city without a new identity or flight target.

No approved photograph · intentional no-photo layout

Overview

Barcelona works well when you plan by neighbourhood: Gaudí buildings in the Eixample, the lanes of the old city and art museums on separate outings. Choose the interiors that matter to you before adding up tickets.

The Gothic Quarter follows the Roman and medieval core. The later Eixample grid gave the expanding city room for the modernista buildings that still shape its streets.

Worth seeing

  • Sagrada Família — See Gaudí’s basilica as a planned interior visit rather than assuming you can walk straight in. Check the official visitor options for your date.
  • Park Güell — Combine Gaudí’s designed spaces with a park outing. Check current works and ticket arrangements before deciding which parts to visit.
  • Casa Batlló — Look for modernista detail on Passeig de Gràcia. An exterior walk and an interior visit are different choices for your budget.
  • Museu Picasso — Use the collection to explore Picasso’s formative years; it offers a different focus from a Gaudí architecture day.
  • Museu Nacional d’Art de Catalunya — Choose this as an alternative art-museum outing. Check the current collection and entry arrangements rather than assuming another museum’s ticket includes it.

Choosing a base

Eixample — Consider a base near a useful metro station for architecture outings and cross-city travel. Gaudí buildings and a street-grid base. Check the exact block and station; Eixample is a large district.

Gothic Quarter — Consider the old city if historic lanes are the main reason for your visit. Exploring the Roman and medieval centre on foot. Check the property entrance, luggage access and noise conditions directly.

Arrival and transport

Arriving at Barcelona–El Prat (BCN) — L9 Sud serves both airport terminals and connects with other metro lines. Compare the connection to your accommodation, not just the airport station. Some ordinary tickets are not valid at the airport metro gates.

Getting between neighbourhoods — Group the Eixample buildings together and plan a separate old-city outing. Park Güell needs its own approach; check TMB’s route information rather than assuming every sight is an easy walk.

2 nights in Barcelona

  • Day 1: Casa Batlló — Start with the exterior and the surrounding Eixample streets if arrival leaves time.
  • Day 2: Sagrada Família — Use your confirmed entry arrangement; leave time to enjoy the interior. Park Güell — Make a separate connection to the park; do not assume the two Gaudí sites are adjacent.
  • Day 3: Museu Picasso — Only with enough time before departure and confirmed access.

3 nights in Barcelona

  • Day 1: Casa Batlló — An exterior architecture walk if arrival leaves time.
  • Day 2: Sagrada Família — Use your confirmed basilica visit. Park Güell — A separate park journey; leave room for a break.
  • Day 3: Museu Picasso — Make this the main art visit.
  • Day 4: Museu Nacional d’Art de Catalunya — An optional second museum only if departure permits; otherwise choose one museum for the stay.

Interior tickets, local journeys and airport travel are separate choices. This guide supplies no reviewed accommodation, meal or complete attraction budget. Check the actual property total and chosen tickets.

Istanbul v3 · DRAFT

Existing approved photograph and identity; replaces attraction-only tram advice with airport-specific arrival planning.

Tolga Tüfekçi · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0 · cropped and resized to WebP; cropped for display · source · licence. Existing approved derivative; no new crop.

Overview

Istanbul’s historic peninsula puts Byzantine and Ottoman landmarks close enough to explore as one area. Keep the major interiors on separate parts of your plan, and choose your base around the airport connection as well as the sights.

Hagia Sophia’s surviving structure dates from the Byzantine period; Topkapı later served as an Ottoman palace and administrative centre. Their different histories are visible within the same compact sightseeing area.

Worth seeing

  • Ayasofya (Hagia Sophia) — Explore the building’s Byzantine and Ottoman history. Sightseeing arrangements and worship access are different; verify the current visitor entrance before going.
  • Topkapı Palace — Visit the former Ottoman administrative centre and palace courtyards. Check the official ticket scope before adding optional sections to your plan.
  • Basilica Cistern — Go underground to see a surviving Byzantine water-storage structure near Hagia Sophia. Treat it as a separate visit, not part of another landmark’s ticket.
  • Blue Mosque (Sultanahmet Camii) — Look for the İznik tilework in this active mosque. Plan sightseeing around worship and follow the visitor guidance on arrival.

Choosing a base

Sultanahmet — Consider this area for a stay centred on the historic peninsula. Keeping the four landmark visits in one part of the city. Airport journeys still need connections; location near a sight does not mean a direct airport ride.

Sirkeci and Eminönü — Compare properties near the T1 corridor if onward tram travel matters to your plan. Moving between the historic sights and the waterfront tram stops. Check the exact stop, walking route and luggage access rather than relying on an area label.

Arrival and transport

Arriving at IST — Istanbul Airport (IST) has metro, Havaist coach and IETT bus options. Choose an onward connection for your exact base; the airport metro is not a direct tram to Sultanahmet. Check current routes before departure.

Arriving at SAW — Sabiha Gökçen (SAW) is a different airport. M4 links it with Kadıköy and connects with Marmaray at Ayrılık Çeşmesi. Plan the onward leg to a European-side base separately.

Around the historic peninsula — T1 serves Sultanahmet, Gülhane, Sirkeci and Eminönü. Use the station that fits the entrance you need, then walk locally. Keep prayer restrictions separate from transport planning.

2 nights in Istanbul

  • Day 1: Blue Mosque (Sultanahmet Camii) — An arrival-day visit only when sightseeing access is open.
  • Day 2: Topkapı Palace — Give the palace the main part of your sightseeing day. Basilica Cistern — Add the separate cistern visit only if the day remains comfortable.
  • Day 3: Ayasofya (Hagia Sophia) — Fit the visitor entrance to your departure time, or swap this with day two.

3 nights in Istanbul

  • Day 1: Blue Mosque (Sultanahmet Camii) — Begin near your base when worship and sightseeing arrangements allow.
  • Day 2: Topkapı Palace — Keep time for the palace rather than rushing through every interior.
  • Day 3: Ayasofya (Hagia Sophia) — Check current tourist access.
  • Day 4: Basilica Cistern — A final separate visit if your departure permits; otherwise move it to day three.

Airport journeys, attraction tickets and accommodation need separate checks. No ferry, taxi or admission allowance is supplied by this guide, and none should be read as included in a complete trip price.

Tokyo v3 · DRAFT

Existing reviewed identity and contrasting east/west sightseeing areas; airport and neighbourhood choices materially affect a short stay.

No approved photograph · intentional no-photo layout

Overview

Tokyo is easier to plan as a few neighbourhood outings than a citywide checklist. Pair Asakusa with the Skytree area, and keep Meiji Jingu and Yoyogi Park together on a different day. Your airport and nearest station matter as much as the district name.

Sensoji anchors Asakusa’s temple streets. Across the city, Meiji Jingu honours Emperor Meiji and Empress Shōken in a planted forest, giving a short stay two very different views of Tokyo’s history.

Worth seeing

  • Sensoji and Nakamise — Approach Sensoji through Kaminarimon and the Nakamise shopping street. Leave room to explore the surrounding Asakusa streets rather than treating the gate as the whole visit.
  • Meiji Jingu — Walk through the shrine forest near Harajuku. Respect worshippers and check the shrine’s current visitor guidance.
  • Yoyogi Park — Take an outdoor break beside the Meiji Jingu area. The park and shrine are separate places, even though they pair well in one outing.
  • Tokyo Skytree — Choose whether you want the paid observation decks or simply the surrounding riverside outing. Check the official ticket options; skyline visibility is not guaranteed.
  • Sumida River Walk — Use the named pedestrian connection between Asakusa and the Skytree side for a river outing. Check current access and choose rail if the walk does not suit you.

Choosing a base

Asakusa — Compare bases near the station if the temple streets and eastern Tokyo are priorities. Sensoji and the Sumida-side outing. Western Tokyo still needs a rail journey; check the airport connection for your terminal.

Shinjuku — Consider a station-linked base for rail connections and the western side of the city. A busy district with dining and transport options. The large station and different exits make the exact address important, especially with luggage.

Arrival and transport

Check HND versus NRT — Haneda (HND) and Narita (NRT) have different rail and airport-bus connections. Compare the service to your actual station or hotel and check the final service for your arrival time. A Tokyo city code does not identify your terminal.

Plan by neighbourhood and station — Keep Asakusa and the Skytree area in the same outing. Use rail for the separate Meiji Jingu–Yoyogi outing near Harajuku. Check the station exit and any access needs before choosing a walking route.

2 nights in Tokyo

  • Day 1: Sensoji and Nakamise — Explore the temple approach only if arrival leaves time.
  • Day 2: Meiji Jingu — Travel to the Harajuku side for a separate outing. Yoyogi Park — Add the adjacent park rather than another cross-city transfer.
  • Day 3: Tokyo Skytree — Choose a deck booking only if your departure leaves enough time.

3 nights in Tokyo

  • Day 1: Sensoji and Nakamise — Keep the first outing around Asakusa.
  • Day 2: Meiji Jingu — Use a separate western-city outing. Yoyogi Park — Add the adjacent park instead of another cross-city transfer.
  • Day 3: Tokyo Skytree — Choose a deck visit or the surrounding area.
  • Day 4: Sumida River Walk — A final eastern-side walk only if it fits your base and airport timing.

No citywide rail pass is assumed to cover every operator, airport service or ticketed sight. Accommodation, meals, airport travel and chosen admissions remain costs to check, not reviewed totals.

Kyoto v3 · DRAFT

Existing gateway identity and small eastern-city cluster; needs explicit ground-arrival guidance and respectful short-stay planning.

No approved photograph · intentional no-photo layout

Overview

Start a short Kyoto stay with the eastern temple streets. Kiyomizu-dera, Yasaka Pagoda and Yasaka-jinja fit a focused outing; with another day, consider a separate rail journey to Fushimi Inari. Respect worship spaces and residential lanes.

Kiyomizu-dera’s hillside setting and timber stage help explain the city’s temple landscape. Gion developed around Yasaka-jinja; its traditional entertainment culture is still part of a living neighbourhood.

Worth seeing

  • Kiyomizu-dera — Visit the hillside temple and its timber stage. The approach is part of the outing; check current access if slopes or steps are a concern.
  • Yasaka-jinja — Explore the shrine at the edge of Gion, with Maruyama Park nearby. Keep worship access and sightseeing etiquette in mind.
  • Hokan-ji (Yasaka Pagoda) — Look for the pagoda among the lanes below Kiyomizu-dera. This plan is for an exterior view and does not assume the building is open inside.
  • Maruyama Park — Use the park east of Yasaka-jinja as a slower outdoor stop. Do not plan around blossom displays without checking the season and current conditions.
  • Fushimi Inari Taisha — Make a separate southern-Kyoto outing to the shrine and torii-lined paths. Choose a walking extent that suits you; the plan does not require a full mountain circuit.

Choosing a base

Gion and southern Higashiyama — Consider a base around the reviewed eastern sights if you want to explore on foot. Temple streets and shorter local outings. Check slopes, luggage access and private-lane rules at the exact property.

Kyoto Station area — Consider this base if airport and intercity connections are the main logistical priority. Access to the station used by airport rail and bus services. The eastern temple area still needs a separate local connection.

Arrival and transport

Arriving through KIX or ITM — From Kansai (KIX), compare Haruka rail and airport-limousine connections. Osaka Itami (ITM) has different bus and rail-change options. Neither airport is in Kyoto: price the actual ground leg separately.

Around Kyoto and the eastern sights — Use the official planner to reach your base. The eastern walk links Kiyomizu-dera, Yasaka Pagoda and Yasaka-jinja; allow for slopes and crowds. Fushimi Inari is a separate southern-Kyoto rail outing.

2 nights in Kyoto

  • Day 1: Yasaka-jinja — Begin near Gion if arrival leaves time. Maruyama Park — Add the adjoining park as an optional outdoor stop.
  • Day 2: Kiyomizu-dera — Make the hillside temple the main visit.
  • Day 3: Hokan-ji (Yasaka Pagoda) — An exterior look on a final local walk, only if it fits departure.

3 nights in Kyoto

  • Day 1: Yasaka-jinja — A gentle start near Gion, respecting worshippers.
  • Day 2: Kiyomizu-dera — Allow time for the hillside approach. Hokan-ji (Yasaka Pagoda) — An exterior view on the same eastern-city outing.
  • Day 3: Fushimi Inari Taisha — Use the rail approach for a separate southern-Kyoto visit.
  • Day 4: Maruyama Park — A final nearby park break only if it fits your base and departure.

The Osaka-airport transfer is a required separate planning item. This guide does not price it, hotel rooms, meals or temple entry; missing amounts must not be treated as zero.

Bangkok v3 · DRAFT

Existing dual-airport identity and compact Old City sights; clear arrival and heat-aware pacing make a useful Southeast Asian short-stay guide.

No approved photograph · intentional no-photo layout

Overview

Bangkok’s royal temples, river crossings and Chinatown work best as a few connected outings. Keep the Old City together, leave breaks between interiors and plan your airport journey from the actual airport code.

The Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew form the royal and religious core of Rattanakosin. Wat Pho sits nearby, while Wat Arun stands across the Chao Phraya: the river is part of the city’s geography, not just a view.

Worth seeing

  • Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew — Make this one planned palace-complex visit. Check current entry arrangements and the dress code before leaving your base.
  • Wat Pho — Explore the temple beside the Grand Palace on a separate ticket. Leave room for the courtyards rather than adding it as a rushed photograph stop.
  • Wat Arun — Plan a separate visit on the opposite bank of the river. Confirm the crossing or rail-and-walk route you will use rather than assuming a ferry timetable.
  • Yaowarat — Explore Chinatown as its own street outing, using Wat Mangkon MRT for a useful approach. Food choices and stall operation vary; there is no fixed meal budget here.

Choosing a base

Old City and the Sanam Chai area — Compare a base here if the palace and temple cluster drives your visit. Fewer cross-city trips for these sights. Check the exact station or pier connection and the property’s luggage access.

Asok and Sukhumvit interchange area — Consider a station-linked base if rail connections are more important than being beside the temples. An interchange between BTS and MRT services. The Old City remains a separate journey; do not assume every Sukhumvit address is near the interchange.

Arrival and transport

Suvarnabhumi (BKK) — The Airport Rail Link runs towards Phaya Thai, with an MRT connection from the Makkasan area. Match the final leg to your base and check the current airport guide for service notices. This is not the same route as Don Mueang.

Don Mueang (DMK) — DMK has Red Line and airport-bus options. A September government advisory described transport disruption: check current operator notices before relying on a service. No taxi availability is assumed.

Old City, river and Chinatown — Use Sanam Chai for Old City planning and Wat Mangkon for Yaowarat. Wat Arun is across the river from the palace area: check the actual crossing or onward route, and allow breaks between temple visits.

2 nights in Bangkok

  • Day 1: Yaowarat — Keep arrival-day plans flexible; choose food individually.
  • Day 2: Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew — Follow the current dress and visitor rules. Wat Pho — Add this nearby temple only if you still want another interior.
  • Day 3: Wat Arun — A separate river-side visit only if departure leaves time.

3 nights in Bangkok

  • Day 1: Yaowarat — A flexible Chinatown outing after settling in.
  • Day 2: Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew — Give the palace complex its own main outing.
  • Day 3: Wat Pho — A slower temple visit; use the rest of the day for breaks.
  • Day 4: Wat Arun — Only if airport timing permits; otherwise pair with day three after confirming the crossing.

Temple tickets, river crossings, airport travel, meals and rooms need individual checks. This guide gives no reviewed food allowance and does not assume a meal or transfer is included in a ticket.
